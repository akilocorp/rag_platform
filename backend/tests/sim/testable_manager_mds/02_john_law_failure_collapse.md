# 02 — John Law, failure, the collapse pair

**Hired:** John Law · **Outcome:** failure · **Private picks:** all three → John Law

| Student | Role |
|---|---|
| Dana | Logistics Manager |
| Ben | Marketing Manager |
| Mei | Operations Manager |

Unanimous from the start, which is the trap: John's five strengths are printed in **all
three** packets, so he looks outstanding to every reader and nobody feels a need to pool.
This is the plain **FAILURE track** run, and the sharpest test of the counting rules.

## Ground truth

| Holder | John Law strengths (identical for all three) | John Law concern |
|---|---|---|
| Dana (Logistics) | leadership program · 15 years at the company · CFA · demanding but fair · overseas finance division | **tends to micromanage subordinates** |
| Ben (Marketing) | *same five* | **passive with more powerful executives** |
| Mei (Operations) | *same five* | **passive with superiors** |

Ben's and Mei's concerns are the case pack's one **collapse pair** — the same passivity
worded twice. John is therefore **5 strengths / 2 concerns**, not 5/3.

"Demanding but fair" (strength, Dana) and "micromanages" (concern, Dana) are a marked
**tension pair**: one behaviour, two framings. ACTR should surface it and refuse to resolve
it. For the unchosen: Jacky Chan 8/1, Jet Li 5/3.

## Personas

- **Dana** — Reflective and a bit defensive; half-thinks she saw it coming. Holds the micromanaging note and the "demanding but fair" strength, and is uneasy that they might be the same thing.
- **Ben** — Fast and agreeable, voted in two minutes and says so. Willing to concede a point immediately when someone else words it better.
- **Mei** — Blunt and economical. Says "same" or "pretty much word for word" rather than restating, which is exactly what makes the collapse pair hard to spot.

## What this tests

| # | Behaviour | Pass | Fail |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Opener on a failure | "Could you have seen that coming?" first — the "why" only after the split is in | Opens with "why did you choose him", which hardens their commitment |
| 2 | Concerns before strengths | Pools John's concerns first, pivots back when they drift to strengths | Lets the room list strengths and never returns |
| 3 | **The collapse pair** | When Ben and Mei give the two passivity wordings, asks whether that is one problem or two | Accepts it as two, or announces "that's one" itself |
| 4 | Counting stays theirs | Counts only what they said aloud; never tops the list up from the pack | States "John has two concerns" before they have said both |
| 5 | One candidate at a time | Finishes John before opening Jet Li or Jacky | Builds a comparison table across all three |
| 6 | **The tension pair** | Surfaces "demanding but fair" against "micromanages" without resolving it | Explains which framing is correct |
| 7 | Never names the best | Never says Jacky was the answer, including at the end | Reveals it once they are close |
| 8 | Reaches F7 | Ends on a written procedure | Ends on ACTR explaining the lesson |
